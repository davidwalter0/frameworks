* 1. DRY — the census counts text; DRY is about decisions

[[file:../ui/docs/duplication-census.org]] measures *textual* duplication because
that is what is cheaply measurable. But "duplicated lines" and "DRY violation"
are different quantities, and conflating them produces two opposite errors.

A DRY violation is *one decision expressed in more than one place*, such that
changing the decision requires finding every expression. Text is a proxy for
that, and a lossy one in both directions.

** Direction 1 — identical text that is NOT a DRY violation

Two files can be byte-identical by *coincidence*: each is the obvious minimal
implementation of a small idea, and neither depends on the other. Merging them
creates a coupling that did not exist, and the cost lands later — when one
consumer needs a change the other must not have.

The test is not "are these the same?" but *"if this decision changes, must both
change?"*

** Direction 2 — different text that IS one DRY violation

The reverse error is more expensive, because textual measurement cannot see it at
all. The family's largest DRY violations are decisions with many *dissimilar*
expressions:

| Decision | Expressions | Textual similarity |
|----------+-------------+--------------------|
| Where does a setting persist, and when is it written? | kit =SettingsStore= + word-bank's own =FileSettingsStore= + voicelab =settings_providers= + 6 more surfaces | low — no two look alike |
| What does "dark mode" mean? | Flutter engine (portal) → =platformBrightness= → kit =_resolveBrightness= → 7 apps' own =ThemeMode= plumbing | near zero |
| How is an app laid out at each width? | =AdaptiveLayout= (kit + task-board) + =resizable_split= (word-bank, voicelab) + =adaptive_panels= (word-bank) + 6 shells | low |
| What is an installable Linux app? | 3 Go installers (now 1) + 3 =lib-appdir.sh= + =.desktop= templates | moderate |

Grepping for identical text finds *none* of these. They surface only by asking
"what decision is this?" — which is why the census's cause taxonomy
(STALE/AHEAD/ORPHAN/ABSENT) sits above its line counts, and why the recurring-shape
analysis in the inventory (§"five recurring shapes") is the more load-bearing
half of that document.

** The guard against over-DRYing

The opposite failure — hoisting too eagerly — is already controlled by the
*two-consumer admission rule* for tier 2
([[file:../ui/docs/framework-subsumption-design.org]] §2), which is the user's
standing position that a package with one consumer is that project's code.

Two corollaries worth stating explicitly, because both were nearly violated this
session:

1. *=gatehub-kit/observe= has exactly one consumer.* It is a good package
   (1,352 source lines, near-1:1 tests, importable, no =internal/=) and the
   temptation to hoist it into =frameworks/go= is strong precisely *because* it is
   good. The rule says leave it and import in place until a second consumer
   exists. Recorded in [[file:../go/docs/go-framework-design.org]] §3.2.
2. *Logging has no duplication at all* — =log/slog= appears in 2 of 11 repos. A
   =frameworks/go/pkg/logging= would be an abstraction with no second consumer
   and no drift to arrest. Deferred, and the deferral is *recorded* rather than
   left implicit, so a later session does not read the absence as an oversight.

* 2. Interface decoupling — the seam convention, already proven 4×

The kit contains a consistent, well-designed platform-decoupling pattern that is
*nowhere documented as a convention*. It is discovered by reading. Extracting and
mandating it is the highest-value interface work available, because it costs
nothing to adopt — it already exists and already works.

** The shape

Four instances, measured:

| Seam | Interface | Selector | Implementations |
|------+-----------+----------+-----------------|
| =host_env= | =host_env.dart= 77 | =host_env_default.dart= 31 | =_io= 41, =_web= 41, =_stub= 33 |
| =host_process= | =host_process.dart= 64 | =host_process_default.dart= 13 | =_io= 53, =_web= 24 |
| =settings_backend= | =settings_backend.dart= 59 | =settings_backend_default.dart= 18 | =_io= 107, =_web= 51, =_stub= 20 |
| =font_enumeration= | (in =font_tools.dart=) | =font_enumeration_default.dart= 12 | =_io= 52, =_web= 18 |

Four files per seam, with one job each:

#+begin_src text
X.dart          the abstract interface. NO platform imports. Pure, testable.
X_default.dart  the selector: a conditional `export` choosing a variant.
X_io.dart       the dart:io implementation.
X_web.dart      the browser implementation.
X_stub.dart     the degraded implementation (present where a host may have neither).
#+end_src

Consumers import only =X_default.dart= and receive the correct variant. Measured
consumers: =widgets/file_ref=, =clipboard/primary_selection=, =anthy/anthy_egg=,
=theme/font_tools=, =theme/host_ui_font=, =settings/settings_store=.

** The principle that makes it work, stated in the kit's own words

From =host_env.dart=:

#+begin_quote
Implementations are trivial on =dart:io= hosts and near-empty on the web, which
is the point: callers get a *total interface* and branch on the *values*, not on
which platform they think they are.
#+end_quote

That is the whole decoupling idea, and it is stronger than the file layout:

- *Total interface.* Every method exists on every host. =home= returns =String?=
  rather than being absent on the web; =environment= returns an empty map rather
  than throwing.
- *Branch on capability, not identity.* =hasProcesses= is a =bool= on the
  interface. A caller asks "can I spawn?" — never "am I on Linux?". Platform
  identity (=HostOs os=) is available but is not the control-flow mechanism.

The second point is what prevents the seam from leaking. A codebase that branches
on =Platform.isLinux= has re-coupled itself to the platform at every call site,
no matter how clean the seam beneath is.

** Where it must be mandated

1. *Tier 2 (=app_kit=).* It has no seams yet because it does not exist. Every
   host-touching capability it grows — window state, tray/indicator, autostart,
   =Inhibit= — takes this shape from the start. The alternative is discovering, as
   the census did for the UI, that nine apps each hand-rolled the same probe.
2. *Go.* The pattern already crossed languages without being named. In
   =pkg/installer=: =Options.Out io.Writer= (output seam),
   =SystemHicolorIndexTheme= as a package var so tests redirect it, and in
   =go-version/pkg/tools/sign.go= the =cosignRunner= seam that makes signing
   testable with no cosign binary. Same idea — an injected boundary with a total
   interface — expressed idiomatically per language. State it once here rather
   than rediscovering it per package.
3. *The capability surface.* =host/capabilities.dart= (223 lines) is the existing
   home for "what can this host do". A new probe belongs there, not as a fresh
   ad-hoc check.

** The interface smell to watch for during migration

A tier-1 component that takes a =WidgetRef=, a =BuildContext= it only uses for a
theme lookup, or a =Platform.isX= branch has failed the boundary. The
=deps-guard= catches the first mechanically; the other two need review. The
=task-board= promotion is the worked example of doing it right: =AdaptiveLayout=
went from =ConsumerWidget= reading a Riverpod notifier to =required this.mode= +
callbacks, and *that refactor is why it could live in the kit at all*.

* 3. Process design and flows — where the design is currently unenforced

This is the section with the finding that most threatens the migration.

** The gates the design depends on

[[file:../ui/docs/framework-subsumption-design.org]] rests on gates: =make check=
green, "98 test files green *unmodified*", "verify against a real artifact", "the
tranche is not done until the fork is deleted". Every one is stated as a
requirement.

** The measured reality: almost none of it is automated

| Repo | CI workflows |
|------+--------------|
| *voicelab* | *3* — =check.yml=, =scan.yml=, =release.yml= |
| ui-kit-private | *0* |
| textloom, word-bank, gatehub-kit, alert-log, mountbridge, mail-sweep, netroute, task-board, notekeep, frameworks | 0 |

*One repo of twelve has CI. The kit — the shared dependency ten apps consume —
has none.* So "the gate" is a local =make check= invoked by whoever is at the
keyboard, with nothing detecting a skip.

Note the irony, consistent with everything else the census found: the family's
only CI lives in =voicelab=, the app with the *lowest* kit adoption and the
*most* forks. Process maturity and code sharing are uncorrelated here.

** Why this is the load-bearing risk, not a nice-to-have

The =task-board= ORPHAN is what an unenforced gate produces. A correct, documented,
two-part plan (2026-07-04) had its promotion half executed and its adoption half
silently dropped. Nothing detected the omission — the doc still says
=[ ] Adoption worktree=, twenty-five days later, and the duplication it created
was found only by this census.

A migration of ~9 tranches across 12 repos, whose central invariant is "the
tranche is not done until the fork is deleted", is *exactly* the shape that fails
this way. The retire step is the step with no artifact: adding shared code
produces a visible commit, deleting a fork produces nothing anyone is waiting
for.

** What good already looks like — copy it, do not invent it

=voicelab/.github/workflows= is an exemplar and should be the template:

- =check.yml= (76 lines) — SHA-pinned actions, runs =make vet lint vuln fmt-check
  test cov=. Directly matches the check-suite contract.
- =scan.yml= (137 lines) — two-job build/scan separation with
  =step-security/harden-runner=, i.e. the secret-exfil-resistant pattern: the
  build job holds credentials, the scan job receives only an artifact.
- =release.yml= (136 lines) — *incomplete*: =actions/checkout@v4=,
  =actions/setup-go@v5= and =subosito/flutter-action@v2= are still tag-pinned,
  each carrying its own =# pin: replace with a full commit SHA= comment. A
  mutable tag on a release workflow is a live supply-chain exposure and the file
  documents its own gap.

** The cross-repo pin flow, and its two defects

=ui-kit-private/tools/pin-monitor/consumers.yaml= is the one piece of
cross-repo process machinery. It lists the shared dep and each consumer's
pubspec. Two defects:

1. *Incomplete.* Six consumers listed; =ghk_dashboard=, =netroute_manager= and =textloom=
   are absent. (=task-board= is correctly absent — it has no dependency.) So the
   monitor under-reports by three, and =textloom= — pinned to a bare sha =5a610a6= —
   is the one most likely to drift. Already tracked in kit todo =62419295=.
2. *Host-local absolute paths, and a feature-branch pin.* Every path is
   =$HOME/go/src/...=, so the file only works on one machine — it cannot
   move into CI as written. And =mountbridge= is pinned to branch
   =disk-backed-lru-persistence=, not =main=, which is either a stale entry or an
   undocumented long-lived divergence.

** The flow to put in place

Ordered by value-per-effort, and deliberately minimal — the goal is to make the
existing gates *observable*, not to build a platform:

1. *=check.yml= on the kit and on =frameworks=, copied from =voicelab=.* Highest
   value by a wide margin: it puts a gate on the shared dependency, which is the
   one place a regression fans out to ten consumers. Now possible for
   =frameworks= because the remote exists.
2. *Finish the =release.yml= pinning* in =voicelab=. Small, and it closes a real
   exposure the file already flags.
3. *Make the retire step produce an artifact.* Each tranche's exit criterion
   names specific files; a trivial script asserting those paths are *gone* turns
   the invisible step into a check. This is the direct control for the ORPHAN
   failure mode.
4. *Relocate and repair =consumers.yaml=* — repo-relative paths, all consumers,
   =main= or an explicit note on why not. It then works in CI.
5. *A cross-repo smoke flow, last.* When tier 1 changes, build each consumer at
   its pin. Genuinely useful, and genuinely the most work; do not let it block
   items 1–4.

** One process property worth preserving deliberately

The copy-first contract makes rollback free *by construction* — abandoning a
tranche means not doing step 4. That is a property of the *flow*, not of anyone's
diligence, and it is the strongest thing in the current process design. Every
change above should preserve it. In particular, do not "simplify" a tranche by
deleting the fork in the same commit that adds the shared code: that trades the
one structural guarantee for a slightly tidier history.

* 4. Summary — the three asymmetries

Each of these is a case where the intuitive reading is backwards, which is why
they are collected here:

1. *Textual duplication and DRY violation are different quantities.* Identical
   text can be coincidence (leave it); dissimilar text can be one decision in
   nine places (fix it). Ask "must both change together?", not "are these the
   same?".
2. *The best-decoupled code in the family is undocumented, and the
   best-automated repo is the worst-shared one.* Both are discoverability
   failures rather than capability failures — the same root cause as the kit's
   =about_dialog= being reimplemented three times, and the reason =ui0= is an
   oracle rather than a gallery.
3. *The step with no artifact is the step that gets skipped.* Adding shared code
   is visible; deleting a fork is not. The ORPHAN proves it empirically at n=1
   with a 25-day detection lag. Gate the deletion, not the addition.
